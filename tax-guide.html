Tax Guidance & Advice

Simple, plain-language explanations to help you understand taxes, filing, and compliance. Tap any card to read more.

Income tax is a tax on the profit or income you earn in a financial year — whether from a salary, a shop, a service, or freelance work. If you run a business, tax is calculated on your net profit (what's left after business expenses), not your total sales.

New Tax Regime Slab Rates:

  • Up to ₹3,00,000 No Tax
  • ₹3,00,000–₹7,00,000 5%
  • ₹7,00,000–₹10,00,000 10%
  • ₹10,00,000–₹12,00,000 15%
  • ₹12,00,000–₹15,00,000 20%
  • Above ₹15,00,000 30%

If your total taxable income is up to ₹7,00,000, a rebate under Section 87A usually brings your tax down to zero.

Slab rates and rebate limits are reviewed periodically — always check the current year's rules or a tax professional before relying on exact figures.

Small business owners and professionals can often reduce paperwork and tax using presumptive taxation:

  • Section 44AD (goods/trading) — deemed profit of just 8% of turnover (6% if payments are received digitally), up to ₹2 crore turnover.
  • Section 44ADA (professionals/freelancers) — deemed profit of 50% of receipts, up to ₹75 lakh turnover.

Genuine business expenses — rent, raw materials, transport, staff wages — also reduce your taxable profit if you're not using presumptive taxation. Individuals under the old tax regime may additionally claim exemptions for things like insurance premiums, certain investments, and home loan interest.

Which deductions apply depends on the regime you choose and your business type — a tax professional can help pick the option that saves you the most.

You generally need to file an Income Tax Return (ITR) if your income is above the basic exemption limit, even if no tax is finally payable — and filing can also help when applying for loans or visas.

Documents you'll usually need:

  • PAN and Aadhaar
  • Bank account statements
  • Sales/income and expense records
  • Form 16 (if salaried) or business income summary

Basic steps: choose the correct ITR form for your income type, total your income and eligible deductions, pay any tax due, file the return online, and complete e-verification.

The filing deadline for most individuals and small businesses (without an audit requirement) is usually 31 July — confirm the exact date each year.

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is charged on the sale of most goods and services. Registration is generally required once your annual turnover crosses:

  • ₹40,00,000 for goods (₹20,00,000 in special-category states)
  • ₹20,00,000 for services (₹10,00,000 in special-category states)

Once registered, you'll need to issue proper GST invoices, charge the correct tax rate on your sales, and file periodic returns (like GSTR-1 for sales and GSTR-3B as a monthly/quarterly summary). Registered businesses can also claim input tax credit on GST paid for business purchases.

Below the threshold, GST registration is optional — but some businesses register anyway to claim input credit or work with larger clients.

A few simple, legal habits can meaningfully lower what you owe:

  • Digital Payments: Presumptive taxation rewards digital receipts with a lower deemed profit rate under Section 44AD.
  • Record Keeping: Keep basic records of sales and expenses, even informally — this supports any deduction or exemption you claim.
  • Udyam Registration: Register on Udyam (MSME) — it's free and can unlock lower-cost credit and scheme benefits.
  • Presumptive Taxation: Use 44AD/44ADA if eligible, to avoid maintaining detailed books of accounts.
  • Advance Tax: Pay on time to avoid interest charges if your yearly tax liability is significant.

These are general pointers, not personalised advice — your best strategy depends on your specific income and business structure.

  • ITR filing: usually 31 July for individuals/businesses not requiring an audit.
  • GSTR-3B: monthly, typically by the 20th (or per your filing frequency).
  • GSTR-1: monthly or quarterly, depending on your turnover and scheme.
  • Advance tax: quarterly instalments — mid-June, mid-September, mid-December, and mid-March — if your total tax liability for the year exceeds ₹10,000.
  • GST annual return (GSTR-9): once a year, for registered businesses above the applicable turnover limit.

Exact dates can shift slightly each year or get extended — always confirm on the official portals closer to the date.

Do I need to register my business if I earn very little?

Not necessarily for GST — registration depends on the turnover thresholds. But it's still worth checking if any free registrations (like Udyam) could help you.

What happens if I don't file my ITR?

You may face late fees, interest on unpaid tax, and difficulty accessing loans or government schemes that ask for filed returns.

Is GST the same as income tax?

No — GST is a tax on sales of goods/services, while income tax is a tax on your overall profit or income for the year. Many small businesses deal with both.

Can I file taxes without an accountant?

Yes, especially with presumptive taxation, many small business owners file on their own using the official e-filing portal — though a professional can help avoid mistakes as your business grows.

Important Notice

This page gives general guidance based on commonly applicable rules in the informal economy. Tax laws are complex and subject to change. For specific advice tailored to your business situation, please consult a qualified tax professional or chartered accountant.
